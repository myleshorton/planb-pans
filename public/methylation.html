Methylation & detox.
The engine no specialist owns.

The most overlooked piece of the whole PANS picture — and the one that explains why some kids get worse on treatments that should help. Methylation and detox are the body’s cleanup engine: how it makes neurotransmitters, processes folate and B12, and clears infections, toxins, and the debris of treatment. When the engine is blocked, killing the bug just floods a body that can’t take out the trash. Here’s how to test it, read it, and support it — including the two tests parents are never told to run.

Why it’s the bottleneck

No single specialist owns this. Neurologists, immunologists, and ID doctors aren’t trained in the biochemistry of clearance — so the methylation/detox engine is the gap that gets missed. But it’s often the reason a kid stalls: if the body can’t methylate and can’t drain, it can’t clear what’s making it sick on its own — and it can’t handle the die-off when you start a kill protocol.

The reframe: killing infections is doing the work for the child. Fixing the engine helps the body clear them itself — and tolerate the treatments that target the rest.

Hair tissue mineral analysis shows minerals, toxic metals, and — the part that matters most — the ratios between them. The key ones:

The honest catch — the test is good, the interpretation is the gap

Most HTMA practitioners are trained by the labs that sell the test and follow the company’s algorithm — kitchen-sink supplement advice that often contradicts your specialists, leaving you unsure who to trust. HTMA is genuinely valuable; the interpretation is where it falls apart.

How to read it right

  • Don’t react to single values. Hair levels swing; look at the ratios and the pattern over time (re-test to see direction), not one number.
  • Pair it with blood. HTMA tells one half of the story; RBC / serum minerals + copper + ceruloplasmin + zinc + the Walsh panel tell the other. Read them as one.
  • Anchor to symptoms. A “high” number with no matching picture is noise; a pattern that explains the behavior is signal.

This is the synthesis no single specialist seems able to do — reading hair + blood + OAT together through the Walsh protocol. It’s exactly what Minta does. Folate + the FRAT test

This is the box almost no one checks — and for a regressed, speech-losing, or OCD-locked kid, it can be the answer.

What FRAT is

FRAT = the Folate Receptor Antibody Test. Some kids make autoantibodies against the folate receptor at the blood-brain barrier — so even when blood folate looks perfectly normal, folate can’t get into the brain (cerebral folate deficiency). Standard folate testing misses it entirely. Run it for every PANS / autism kid, especially with regression, speech loss, or relentless OCD.

If it’s positive

  • Leucovorin (folinic acid) bypasses the blocked receptor and gets folate into the brain — results can be dramatic.
  • A dairy-free trial can lower the antibodies (they cross-react with a milk protein) — a cheap first move.

Safety — do not freelance this

Do NOT take leucovorin unless you’re FRAT-positive and actually need it. Given when it’s not needed, or pushed too fast, it can cause real flare-ups. Low and slow, with your practitioner.

The research

  • Frye’s team found folate-receptor autoantibodies in 70 of 93 autistic children tested. Frye, MD/PhD — Cerebral Folate Deficiency
  • A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial: folinic acid significantly improved verbal communication in autistic children with language impairment. Frye et al., Molecular Psychiatry
  • Meta-analysis: children with ASD were ~19× more likely to carry a folate-receptor autoantibody than typically-developing kids. Rossignol & Frye, J. Pers. Med. 2021

The FRAT is run through specialty labs (e.g., Religen). Cliff notes: blood folate normal ≠ brain folate normal. Test the antibodies; if positive, leucovorin (carefully) can change everything.

The methylation framework

Methylation is a cycle — produce, transport, use, and clear methyl groups that run detox, neurotransmitters, and gene expression. Three teachers map it, and they agree more than they differ:

  • Walsh — the phenotype that’s actually expressing (whole-blood histamine + copper/zinc): over- vs under-methylation, copper overload, pyrrole.
  • Yasko — the staged, nucleotide-supported SNP protocol (RNA support, methyl donors low and slow).
  • Lynch (StrateGene) — the pathway-stage model that turns raw SNPs into a readable map.

Start cheap

23andMe ($99) + StrateGene (free upload) covers every major methylation SNP — MTHFR, COMT, CBS, MAO — the same ones the $400 panels report. The expensive panels buy curation, not more data.

Food first. Before megadosing methyl donors (which can backfire in over-methylators), the gentlest lever is methylation-supporting food — leafy greens, legumes, the “epinutrients” (Kara Fitzgerald’s work). Match the support to the phenotype; more is not better.

Detox = the clearance engine

This is the rule that explains the most heartbreaking moments in PANS treatment:

When a kill protocol (antibiotics, herbs) makes your child WORSE — behaviors flare, not fade — it usually isn’t the wrong treatment. The clearance engine can’t keep up with the die-off: methylation and the drainage pathways are blocked, so the endotoxin and neurotoxin load overwhelms a body that can’t take out the trash. The killing is working — the exits aren’t open.

What to do

  • Step back from the kill and open the exits first: methylation support (matched to the phenotype), gentle binders (kid-appropriate / food-based for sensitive kids), lymph, hydration, bowel + liver.
  • Then resume, low and slow. The same treatment is usually tolerable once the body can drain.
  • This is the same logic as Shoemaker’s mold sequencing (drainage before/with the kill) — the exits come first.

Constipation — the exit that has to stay open

Before anything else: get your child pooping — big, complete bowel movements, every single day. It is the first thing to fix and the most important drainage pathway, and most PANS kids are backed up. A constipated child reabsorbs the very toxins and die-off they’re trying to clear — so detox stalls, the load builds, and behaviors flare. Non-negotiable, before and during any kill protocol.

And look upstream — mold can shut the gut down. Mycotoxins inhibit gut motility: in one mold-exposed household, 3 of 5 people couldn’t go. If a child suddenly stops pooping, suspect the environment, not just the diet.

The hard rule: no kill herbs — or any antimicrobial — until your child is pooping daily. If the bowels aren’t moving, the die-off has nowhere to go, and you just poison a backed-up body (that’s the flare). Open this exit first. Then start the kill.

The Plan B way — gentle, mineral-first, root-cause

  • Magnesium — first-line, daily. Nancy O’Hara favors magnesium taurate (~1,500 mg/day) — well-absorbed and calming (double duty for the nervous system), titrated up to bowel tolerance (the dose that gives a soft daily stool). Magnesium glycinate is a gentle alternative.
  • Magnesium citrate — emergencies only. It’s the stronger osmotic/laxative form — reserve it for an acute, stuck episode, not daily use.
  • Water + soluble fiber + food: real hydration · ground flax / chia · prunes, pears, kiwi · cooked vegetables.
  • Vitamin C to bowel tolerance · daily movement · abdominal massage · castor-oil packs · a squatty-potty.
  • Fix the root: gut dysbiosis, low thyroid, magnesium deficiency, poor bile flow, and dysmotility all drive constipation — treat those, don’t just push stool.

What Plan B does NOT use: Miralax (PEG 3350) — there are real parent-reported neuro/behavioral concerns in sensitive kids — senna and other stimulant laxatives, and drugstore OTC laxatives. Gentle, mineral-first, and root-cause — not a quick chemical fix off the CVS shelf.
