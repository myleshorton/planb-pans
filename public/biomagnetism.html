Biomagnetism.
Magnets, and a mystery.

The one on this whole map that can honestly feel like witchcraft — and the one where the gap between “there’s no proof” and “I watched it work” is widest. Here’s the honest version: what it is, what the evidence does (and doesn’t) show, the risks, how to find the right practitioner, and how to keep clear eyes while staying open.

What it is

Biomagnetic Pair Therapy (BPT) was developed by Dr. Isaac Goiz Durán in Mexico in the 1980s. The theory: pathogens settle where the body’s pH is off — some in acidic spots, some in alkaline ones — and pairs of those imbalanced points sit at predictable places on the body. A practitioner places medium-strength magnets at those paired points to nudge the local pH back toward neutral, which (the theory goes) makes the tissue inhospitable to the bug.

It comes in very different forms, and that difference matters more than anything:

Choose your path

Same as everything in this map: it bends to your child. Tap to open.

1 · Is your child sensitive / reactive?

Yes (most PANS kids) → start with the gentler one-pathogen-at-a-time Joan Randall Protocol, not an all-at-once approach.
The all-at-once styles (LymeStop, Dr. Garcia) are powerful but rough — they can overwhelm a reactive child.

2 · Behaviors getting worse during sessions?

Same rule as any kill modality: worsening usually means the clearance engine can’t keep up. Step back and open the exits first — methylation + detox/drainage (gentle binders, lymph, hydration). Then resume, slower.

3 · Behaviors improved — so is the infection gone?

Don’t assume. One family watched rage and constipation lift almost immediately — while the same child’s Lyme bloodwork actually rose. The relationship to measured pathogen load is genuinely unclear. Retest before you call it cleared.

4 · It’s not the whole answer — what else?

Biomag is one tool, not a cure-all. Pair it with the real workup (full infection panel, immune, drainage), and if it stalls, the rest of the menu is there: antibiotics, herbals, phage, hyperthermia, bee venom, SOT.

The honest evidence

There are no formal studies. The evidence is entirely anecdotal. Conventional medicine dismisses the mechanism, and the pH/pair theory has not been validated. We are not going to pretend otherwise.

The honest caveat that matters most: symptoms can shift fast, but that doesn’t mean the bug is gone. One family saw rage + constipation lift almost immediately — yet the child’s Lyme levels on bloodwork went up afterward. So retest; don’t assume the infection cleared just because behavior improved.

And yet — the behavioral effect is hard to dismiss. It can feel like witchcraft, and the practitioners draw the same skepticism as LymeStop. But across many kids, aggression in particular seems to melt away with the magnets — repeatedly, observably, even when the labs don’t budge.

The “how” is genuinely baffling — no one can explain the mechanism. That’s not a reason to dismiss it; it’s a reason to study it. Plan B wants to run cohort studies — capture before/after across many kids (behaviors and verified labs) — to settle whether this is coincidence or something real. If you’ve done biomag, log your outcome — that data is how we find out.

The risks

  • Sensitive kids + all-at-once styles = the main risk. The powerful “clear it all” approaches can make a reactive child worse. Start gentle, one pathogen at a time.
  • Behavior improving is not proof of clearance. Labs can stay high — or rise. Retest rather than stop a needed medical workup.
  • Not a substitute for treating an acute infection or for the real diagnostic workup. It’s an adjunct, not the foundation.

How to actually access it

  • Find a practitioner who follows the Joan Randall Protocol (Goiz BPT, one pathogen at a time) — preferably trained through Level 5. That training depth is the closest thing to a quality marker in a field with no licensing.
  • Then check the reviews. Plan B doesn’t bless individuals — we point you to the method and (soon) to real parent verification. A practitioner with universal bad reviews is a skip, no matter the certificate.
  • Sessions are typically in-person, hands-on, 60–90 minutes, over a course of visits. Ask up front whether they’re using a standard protocol or an experimental one for your child’s profile — and how they handle a sensitive kid.

Healer or quack? Judge the behavior.

A genuine healer can look like a quack, and a quack can look like a genuine healer. Biomag looks fringe — magnets, no studies — so it’s exactly the place not to judge by polish or how strange it sounds. Judge the behavior and the incentives.

Red flags (no matter how legit it looks)

  • Claims to cure, or to clear everything in one go and guarantee it.
  • Tells you the labs don’t matter / talks you out of retesting or your other care.
  • Uses fear and urgency to sell more sessions.
  • Won’t adapt for a sensitive kid, or dismisses a worsening reaction.
  • Discourages second opinions.

Green flags (even when it’s fringe)

  • Honest that there’s no proof — calls it what it is.
  • Encourages you to retest and keep your medical workup going.
  • Adapts for a reactive child; backs off when it’s too much.
  • Doesn’t overpromise and welcomes your other doctors.
  • Trained deeply in the method (Level 5) and will talk about cases that didn’t work.
