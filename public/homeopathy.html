What it is — and the three kinds

Homeopathy treats with extremely dilute remedies matched to the whole person. The mainstream view is skeptical (more below) — but it has a long track record of helping kids the standard system gave up on, so Plan B treats it as unproven, not disproven. The first thing to know: “homeopathy” is really three different practices.

KindHow it works — and how to spot it
Classical
start here		One remedy at a time, matched to the whole child, given as a single dose — then you wait and watch for weeks. The rigorous, original method (Hahnemann’s Organon), and the one professional certification actually tests.
Modern
caution		The same remedy (or combinations) dosed DAILY, often at high potency, matched to the condition. The daily dosing is the tell: true classical waits 4–6 weeks between doses; “modern classical” doses daily. Faster for the prescriber — a drift from the original method.
Intuitive / energetic
last resort		The remedy is “sensed” — pendulum dowsing, muscle-testing, or a machine. The selection method is the issue here (see the research below).

Absolutely start with classical. It’s the gentlest and the most rigorous. Done right, it can make real, impactful moves — quietly, in the background, alongside everything else.

Stop the remedy, reassess the choice, and stay with the most subtle single low dose. This is real and it can do harm (the belladonna story below).

The training gap no one tells you about

In most US states, anyone can legally call themselves a “homeopath.” There is no license required and no federal regulation — so quality runs from a person who took one weekend course to one who completed a 1,000+ hour, multi-year program. Parents don’t know to ask. NCCIH/NIH · Univ. of Minnesota patient guide

The credentials that actually mean something

  • CCH — Certified Classical Homeopath (the main US credential). Council for Homeopathic Certification: 1,000 hours (500 theory + 500 supervised clinical), 10 supervised cases, college-level Anatomy/Physiology + Pathology, and a national exam. Explicitly classical. CHC eligibility
  • DABHM (formerly DHt) — for MDs / DOs board-certified in homeopathy. American Board of Homeopathic Medicine
  • DHANP — for naturopaths (NDs) who went deep on classical homeopathy. A generic “ND” is not the same. HANP

How it can harm — “proving” a remedy

People assume homeopathy is “too weak to hurt.” Not true. Give the wrong remedy, or dose it too high or too often (the modern approach), and a child can “prove” it — develop brand-new, strange behaviors they’ve never shown. That’s the remedy proving, not the PANS — and it’s easy to mistake for the illness getting worse.

A real example: one child on high-dose belladonna shut down completely — hid in a dark room, couldn’t function. That was the proving, not the disease. The wrong, high-dose “modern” approach made things worse, not better.

This is why the most subtle form — a single low dose, especially at the start — is the safest. The whole difference comes down to dosing: true classical is ONE remedy, then you wait 4–6 weeks and watch. “Modern classical” is dosing that SAME remedy DAILY (often at high potency) instead of waiting — it can look similar, but daily dosing is NOT true classical. True classical homeopaths consider it risky, with a catch that’s hard to undo: once a child has been dosed that way, gentle true classical often doesn’t work as well afterward. So start true classical first — you can always escalate, but you can’t easily walk it back.

The research — honest both ways

  • The mainstream is skeptical. NIH’s NCCIH states there’s “little evidence to support homeopathy as effective” for most conditions, and that its key concepts “are not consistent with fundamental concepts of chemistry and physics.” We’re not going to pretend otherwise. NCCIH/NIH
  • The intuitive method specifically fails a clean test. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (McCarney et al., J. R. Soc. Med., 2002), homeopaths trying to tell a remedy from placebo by dowsing were right just 48.1% of the time — chance (P=0.689). This is the cleanest reason to be wary of pendulum/muscle-test selection. PubMed 11934908
  • And yet. There are no studies comparing classical-vs-modern outcomes or quantifying the training-quality effect — that research simply doesn’t exist. So the case for a deeply-trained classical homeopath rests on competence, safety, and not skipping the real medical workup — plus the lived reality that, for some children, the right remedy has changed everything.

Plan B’s honest stance: homeopathy is unproven by current science, and it is not a substitute for the workup a PANS kid needs. But it is not nothing — it has brought real kids back, and it deserves study, not reflexive dismissal.

How to find a true classical homeopath

Start at the CHC registry — every practitioner listed holds the CCH (Certified Classical Homeopath) credential, and you can search by location, specialty (including pediatrics), and virtual visits (~580 listed). homeopathcertification.org/find-a-homeopath · for an MD/DO see ABHM, for an ND see HANP.

Then run this checklist

  • “How many years & hours did you train?” (multi-year / ~1,000+ hrs; a weekend = stop)
  • “Are you CCH-certified?” (or DABHM / DHANP) — then verify it
  • “Did you train at an ACHENA-accredited school?”
  • “Do you practice classical — one remedy at a time?”
  • “How do you choose the remedy?” (case-taking + repertory = good; pendulum / muscle-test / a machine = red flag)
  • “How long is the first appointment?” (classical first visits run an hour-plus; a 15-minute intake is a tell)
  • “Will you coordinate with our physician?” (a credible homeopath never asks you to drop medical care)

Healer or quack? It’s harder than it looks.

A genuine healer can look like a quack, and a quack can look like a genuine healer. Don’t judge by how fringe homeopathy sounds — judge the behavior and the incentives.

Red flags

  • Trained a weekend but takes complex kids; won’t name their hours or credential.
  • Picks remedies by pendulum, muscle-testing, or a machine.
  • Daily high-potency dosing called “classical.”
  • Tells you to stop your medical workup or promises a cure.
  • Uses fear or urgency; discourages second opinions.

Green flags

  • CCH-certified, 1,000+ hours, ACHENA-trained — and happy to prove it.
  • One remedy at a time, single dose, then patient watching.
  • An hour-plus first visit; honest that homeopathy is unproven and not a replacement for the workup.
  • Coordinates with your doctors and welcomes second opinions.

Bottom line

Homeopathy is dismissed by the mainstream and unproven — and it has still brought real children all the way back. The move isn’t to believe or scoff; it’s to start classical, vet the training hard, use the gentlest single dose, run it in the background after the big drivers are addressed, and never let it replace the real workup.
